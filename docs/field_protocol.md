# Field Protocol

## Pilot before formal collection

Before formal data collection, the collector records approximately 50 rolls from one machine. The pilot is excluded from the study dataset.

Use the pilot to confirm:

- whether the machine history display shows enough recent outcomes to verify logs;
- whether the displayed dice can be reliably recorded as two faces;
- whether every logged outcome can be reconciled with the machine history display;
- the approximate roll rate and practical duration of a 125-roll session;
- venue rules concerning note-taking, phones, and video.

If a history display is unavailable, do not proceed with formal single-recorder sessions until the group has documented a replacement verification procedure.

## Formal session procedure

1. Record only one machine at a time and start each session on a come-out roll.
2. Record the displayed result for every roll, not an estimate from watching dice in motion.
3. Record the game phase immediately before each roll.
4. After every 10 rolls, compare the log with the machine history display and mark corrections.
5. Recover a missed roll only from the display history. If recovery is impossible, record `gap`; do not guess.
6. End the block if the machine is serviced, dice are changed, the display becomes unusable, or recording is materially interrupted.

## Financial and privacy boundaries

- The collector uses only the pre-agreed table-minimum pass-line bet; do not vary stakes because of outcomes.
- Do not record names, faces, financial details, or video of other people.
- Do not continue logging in conflict with casino staff instructions or venue policy.
